# Contractor Interview & Pilot-Validation Kit
## For the India-first construction material and cost intelligence MVP

Date: 18 September 2026
Purpose: Test whether contractors have a recurring procurement problem, possess usable data, will use the workflow weekly and will pay for it.

## 1. The validation rule

Do not ask whether contractors “like the idea.”

Validate four things:

1. The problem occurs frequently.
2. The problem has measurable financial or time impact.
3. The contractor already has documents/data that can support a solution.
4. The economic buyer will pay for a pilot or continued use.

The first product hypothesis is:

> Small and mid-sized residential contractors lose margin and time because BOQs, quotes, purchases, deliveries and stock records are disconnected.

The first product should help them compare real delivered cost, plan material purchases, find variances and identify supply risks.

## 2. Who to recruit

### Ideal pilot company

- Residential contractor or design-build firm
- 5–30 active projects or repeat projects
- One city or nearby region
- At least three recurring material categories
- Uses Excel, WhatsApp, paper records or basic accounting/project software
- Has a named person responsible for procurement
- Has at least six weeks of historical purchase activity
- Willing to share anonymized documents
- Has an owner or project head available for weekly review

### Recruit 25–30 people for discovery

Suggested mix:

| Persona | Target |
|---|---:|
| Contractor owners/project directors | 12 |
| Site engineers/procurement coordinators | 6 |
| Architects/civil engineers serving contractors | 4 |
| Suppliers/distributors | 3 |
| Developer/GC procurement professionals | 2 |
| Demolition/reuse operators for future learning | 2 |
|
### Select 4–6 firms for pilot

Do not select only friendly companies. Include:

- one digitally organized firm;
- two average spreadsheet/WhatsApp firms;
- one highly manual firm;
- one firm with multiple active sites;
- one firm with a strong procurement owner.

## 3. Recruitment message

### Short WhatsApp/LinkedIn message

> Hello [Name], I am researching how residential contractors manage BOQs, supplier quotes, material purchases and delivery records. I am not selling anything in this conversation. I would like to understand your current process and, if useful, review an anonymized BOQ or supplier quote. The discussion will take 30–45 minutes. Would you be open to a short conversation?

### Pilot invitation after a good interview

> Based on what you shared, I am building a small pilot that converts BOQs and supplier quotes into a material comparison, procurement plan and variance report. It will not place orders or make payments. We would use one or two live projects, keep you in control of every decision and measure time saved, purchase variance and delivery issues. Would you be open to a paid or deposit-backed 6–8 week pilot?

Do not promise savings before the baseline is measured.

## 4. Interview preparation

Before each interview, record:

- Interview ID
- Company name or anonymized code
- City/region
- Interviewee role
- Number of active projects
- Typical project type
- Approximate project size
- Current tools
- Interview date
- Interviewer
- Permission to record notes
- Permission to receive anonymized artifacts

Do not request passwords, private credentials, bank details or unnecessary personal data.

## 5. Interview script

### Opening script

> Thank you for speaking with me. I am trying to understand how construction teams currently plan and purchase materials. I am not going to pitch the product at the beginning. I would like to hear about your most recent real project and the last time a material purchase caused a problem. I may ask to see anonymized documents, but you can decline anything.

### Section A — company and workflow

1. What type of construction projects do you normally handle?
2. How many projects are active right now?
3. Who prepares the BOQ or estimate?
4. Who approves material purchases?
5. Who compares suppliers?
6. Who receives and checks the material on site?
7. Which tools do you use: Excel, WhatsApp, paper, accounting software or project software?
8. How many people touch one material purchase from requirement to receipt?

### Section B — recent real purchase

Ask them to describe the last purchase, not an ideal process.

1. Which material was purchased?
2. What triggered the purchase?
3. Where did the requirement come from?
4. How was the quantity decided?
5. How many suppliers were contacted?
6. How did suppliers send quotes?
7. Did quotes use the same units, pack sizes and specifications?
8. How did you compare price, GST, freight and delivery time?
9. Who made the final decision?
10. How long did this process take?
11. What happened after the material arrived?
12. Was the quantity correct?
13. Was the quality or specification correct?
14. Was there any delay, emergency purchase, rejected material or unused stock?
15. What did this problem cost in money, time or delay?

### Section C — repeated pain

1. Which materials create the most problems?
2. How often do emergency purchases happen?
3. How often is material over-ordered?
4. How often does actual consumption differ from the BOQ?
5. How often are supplier quotes difficult to compare?
6. How often does a delivery arrive late or incomplete?
7. How do you know whether material is already available at another site?
8. What information does the owner usually discover too late?
9. What report would change a purchasing decision this week?
10. What process do you currently use to avoid these problems?

### Section D — current alternatives

1. Have you tried project-management or procurement software?
2. What worked?
3. What did people stop using?
4. Why did they stop using it?
5. What information takes the most manual effort to enter?
6. What would site staff refuse to use?
7. Would a spreadsheet-based workflow be easier to start with?

### Section E — trust and safety

1. What would you need to verify before trusting a recommendation?
2. Which decisions must always remain with an engineer, architect or owner?
3. Would you allow AI to suggest a supplier?
4. Would you allow AI to suggest a material alternative?
5. What documents would you need to see beside a recommendation?
6. Which data could be shared with a software company?
7. Which data must never leave your company?

### Section F — willingness to pilot and pay

Ask only after understanding the workflow.

1. If the system produced a weekly material-control report, who would review it?
2. Which decision would the report need to improve to be valuable?
3. What would stop your team from using it every week?
4. Could you provide one anonymized BOQ, three supplier quotes and recent purchase/delivery records?
5. Could you test it on one live project for six to eight weeks?
6. Would you pay a small pilot fee or refundable deposit for a serious trial?
7. Who would approve that payment?
8. What budget category would it come from?
9. What result would make you continue after the pilot?

Do not introduce a price first. If they volunteer a range, record it. Otherwise test real payment later rather than relying on a survey answer.

## 6. Artifact request

Request only what is necessary and allow anonymization.

### Minimum discovery package

- One historical BOQ or estimate
- Three supplier quotes for one category
- One purchase order or purchase message
- Two delivery challans or invoices
- One example of a material shortage, over-purchase or delayed delivery

### Pilot package per company

- 3–5 historical BOQs
- 1–2 current project BOQs
- 6–8 weeks of purchase records
- 3–5 quotes for major categories
- 20–30 delivery challans/invoices where available
- Project stage and planned completion date
- Existing material dictionary or supplier list
- Optional stock/consumption data

### Anonymization instructions

Remove or mask:

- homeowner names;
- phone numbers;
- personal addresses where not needed;
- bank details;
- tax IDs unless specifically required and protected;
- signatures;
- private contract terms;
- unrelated customer information.

Keep fields needed for analysis:

- material description;
- specification/grade;
- unit;
- quantity;
- price;
- tax;
- freight;
- supplier;
- quote date;
- delivery date;
- received quantity;
- rejected quantity;
- project stage.

## 7. Interview scorecard

Score each company from 0 to 3.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem frequency | Never | Rare | Monthly | Weekly or more |
| Financial impact | None | Unknown | Noticeable | Direct margin/cash-flow impact |
| Current workaround | None needed | Simple | Manual and frustrating | Heavy recurring effort |
| Data availability | None | Scattered | Some usable files | Consistent artifacts |
| Decision-maker access | No access | Influencer only | Can introduce buyer | Buyer directly involved |
| Urgency | Not urgent | Later | Within quarter | Within 30 days |
| Artifact willingness | Refuses | Maybe later | Some artifacts | Provides sample package |
| Pilot willingness | No | Interested only | Unpaid pilot | Paid/deposit-backed pilot |

### Qualification rule

Advance a company to pilot consideration only if:

- total score is at least 17/24;
- problem frequency is at least 2;
- data availability is at least 2;
- artifact willingness is at least 2;
- decision-maker access is at least 2.

Advance the product hypothesis only if at least eight independent companies describe the same recurring problem and at least five provide usable artifacts or agree to provide them.

## 9. Pilot structure

### Pilot duration

6–8 weeks after a 1–2 week baseline.

### Recommended cohort

4–6 contractors/design-build firms in one geography.

### Pilot roles

- One owner/project director per firm
- One site/procurement user per firm
- One implementation contact from your team
- Optional QS/engineer reviewer

### Pilot phases

#### Week 0–2: baseline and onboarding

- Collect artifacts.
- Create the material dictionary.
- Normalize historical records.
- Record current time spent, emergency purchases, delays and price variance.
- Choose one or two live projects per firm.
- Define the success metrics before showing results.

#### Week 3: assisted launch

- Produce the first report manually or semi-automatically.
- Review every material mapping with the user.
- Record corrections.
- Do not send RFQs or purchase orders automatically.

#### Weeks 4–6: live workflow

- Process weekly quotes and purchase updates.
- Record receipts and delays.
- Send weekly owner report.
- Hold a 20-minute review call each week.

#### Weeks 7–8: ROI and renewal

- Compare baseline with pilot period.
- Separate realized savings from avoided or estimated savings.
- Interview adopters and non-adopters.
- Ask for paid continuation.

## 10. Pilot operating workflow

```text
Monday: collect new BOQs, quotes and purchase updates
Tuesday: normalize and review ambiguous lines
Wednesday: calculate delivered cost and procurement queue
Thursday: user confirms recommendations and receipt data
Friday: owner receives exception report
Next week: measure decisions, outcomes and corrections
```

### Human-in-the-loop rules

- User approves ambiguous material mappings.
- User approves grade/specification matches.
- User approves any supplier comparison used for a purchase.
- User approves every external RFQ or message.
- Professional review is required for engineering or safety-critical substitutions.
- The system never claims that a material is structurally safe based only on text or images.

### Cost

### Quantity

## 12. Pilot scorecard and formulas

### Core calculations

```text
Effective delivered cost
= item price + GST + freight + handling - discount

Net material requirement
= approved BOQ quantity - usable stock - open-order quantity

Price variance
= actual delivered cost - baseline/comparison delivered cost

Quantity variance
= ordered or consumed quantity - approved planned quantity

Delivery delay
= actual receipt date - promised delivery date

Realized price savings
= comparable baseline cost - actual cost
```

Do not claim savings if scope, quantity, timing or material grade changed without adjustment.

### Weekly product metrics

- % of lines mapped, needs review or rejected
- Mapping correction rate
- % of quote lines with complete tax/freight/delivery data
- Time from upload to usable report
- Weekly active firms
- Weekly active site users
- Receipt-entry completion rate
- Report open/review rate
- Number of decisions changed by the report

### Economic metrics

- Addressable material spend
- Realized price savings
- Emergency purchase reduction
- Admin/procurement hours saved
- Stockout reduction
- Delivery-delay reduction
- Excess/unused material reduction

Report realized cash savings separately from estimated avoided delay or future risk.

## 13. Go/no-go decision rules

### Continue to paid product if

- At least two firms pay for continuation.
- At least four of six firms submit data weekly by week five.
- At least three firms make a documented purchasing decision using the workflow.
- At least 90% of the top 20 material mappings are correct after review.
- The workflow saves at least four hours per week or reduces addressable material spend by at least 1.5%.
- There is no increase in stockouts, rejected material, rework or delays.
- Users trust the evidence trail.

### Change the product if

- Users want project tracking but not procurement intelligence.
- Users will provide reports but not source documents.
- Supplier quotes are too sparse for comparison.
- Material names/specifications cannot be normalized reliably.
- Owners value visibility but site users refuse data entry.
- The system requires too much manual work to deliver the report.

### Stop or change the wedge if

- Fewer than five target companies describe the same recurring problem.
- Fewer than five can provide usable artifacts.
- No buyer is willing to pay or place a deposit.
- Users cannot identify a decision changed by the product.
- The product creates safety, quality or delay risk.

## 14. Pilot consent and data agreement checklist

Before receiving documents, confirm in writing:

- What documents are being shared
- Which projects are included
- Whether data is anonymized
- Who can access the data
- Whether data may be used for product improvement
- Whether customer data may be used for model training
- Retention period
- Deletion process
- Whether results can be used anonymously in a case study
- That the system is decision support, not structural/legal/approval advice
- That the contractor remains responsible for purchase approval
- That no order, payment or external message will be sent without approval

Do not use customer documents for model training unless the permission and legal basis are clear.

## 15. Weekly pilot review questions

Ask the owner:

1. What decision did the report help you make this week?
2. Which recommendation was wrong or incomplete?
3. Which field took too much effort to provide?
4. Did the report prevent an emergency purchase, delay or excess order?
5. Which supplier information was missing?
6. Did the site user actually use the workflow?
7. What would make you stop using it?
8. Would you pay to continue? Why or why not?

Ask the site user:

1. What was easiest to enter?
2. What was annoying or repetitive?
3. Which material mapping was wrong?
4. Did the system understand local names and units?
5. Could you use it from a phone at the site?
6. What should be removed?

## 16. Recommended first action

Start with ten discovery interviews, not a large software build.

For each company, request:

- one anonymized BOQ;
- three supplier quotes for one material category;
- two recent delivery or invoice records;
- one example of a shortage, delayed delivery, over-purchase or emergency purchase.

Proceed to a concierge pilot if at least five companies provide usable artifacts and at least eight describe the same recurring procurement problem.

The first pilot should prove one narrow claim:

> The system helps a contractor make a better material-purchasing decision every week than their current Excel/WhatsApp process.
